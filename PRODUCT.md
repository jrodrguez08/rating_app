# Product

## Purpose and audience

Rating App gives a small football supporter community a fair, simple way to rate the people who shaped a match. The first audience is a WhatsApp group of Club Sport Herediano supporters in Costa Rica.

## Current foundation milestone

Implemented now: a mobile-first shell with an original 16-bit sports-game visual language, Herediano as the initial configured club, accessible Spanish/English localization, generic domain types, and Firestore-backed football persistence. Manual and scheduled server-side API-Football synchronization can discover fixtures on the persisted 12-hour interval independently of whether another fixture is already known, focus on the relevant operational match, capture a trusted tracked-Team lineup/head-coach snapshot during the final pre-kickoff hour when available, retry that focused capture conservatively during play when complete evidence is still missing, reconcile exact participation after `FT`, and establish an idempotent two-hour future voting window only when rating-ready. When provider final status is delayed, the owner can instead attest the actual final whistle and open voting from complete fixture-bound operator-verified evidence through the protected admin flow. Firebase Anonymous Authentication quietly establishes and persists the canonical voter UID without a login screen. During that trusted window, the Home call to action opens a complete player-and-head-coach ballot; one validated, immutable ballot is stored per match and Firebase UID.

`/standings` presents the current domestic league table from a persisted daily snapshot. It is explicitly periodic rather than live, highlights the configured club by stable provider Team ID, and keeps the last valid table visible when a later provider read is empty, malformed, or unavailable. Page rendering never calls the football provider.

While that trusted voting window is open, Home also offers a compact secondary WhatsApp action that shares the canonical rating deep link without voter, ballot, provider, or tracking data.

While a tracked match is live, Home and match detail show a compact chronological summary of persisted confirmed goals when available. Home/away placement mirrors the scoreboard and reflects the latest lifecycle refresh rather than a real-time feed.

At trusted close time, the same lifecycle creates one immutable aggregate summary and closes the rating state. Home and `/matches/{matchId}/results` then reveal community averages, total ballots, player-only co-MVPs, and the separate coach result. `/matches` keeps one relevant match in a featured position and provides compact, tabbed upcoming/recent archive browsing with lifecycle-aware actions, scores, and detail pages. `/players` and `/players/{playerId}` provide a durable supporter-rating catalog, team ranking, and per-match history derived only from those published closed results. Persisted provider fixtures remain useful even when Rating App never opened voting for them; confirmed provider goal events appear as a focused Team/scorer/minute summary when available.

Published result pages also expose one compact sharing area. Supporters can share or copy the canonical result URL, while compatible social platforms receive a match-specific 1200×630 preview with the published score and the first three Players in the existing result order. A separate 1080×1920 image is available through direct download or an ordinary view link for manual Instagram Story posting. Both links remain directly activated by the supporter and do not depend on asynchronous file sharing or pop-up behavior. Both images use the current immutable published summary, explicit MVP set, reviewed canonical Player aliases, and safe persisted photos; they never read ballots or call the football provider. Reopened, unpublished, zero-vote, incomplete, and nonexistent results expose no podium image or sharing controls.

This release adds a public `/studio` presentation surface once its reviewed index and application deployment complete. It selects from the latest 20 final published results and offers a podium, full ranking, and match radiography with match score, Player and coach ratings, ballot count, and the arithmetic mean of the published per-Player match averages. Its fullscreen presentation keeps those three views and an exit action in a compact reserved toolbar without changing the selected Match or view. The stable `/studio/overlay/{matchId}` OBS browser-source URL remains a non-interactive podium and reuses the same published-result projection, canonical Player aliases, MVP semantics, safe photos, and visibility guard as the result page. Studio never reads ballots, calls API-Football, changes a Match, or publishes an unavailable result. It is a viewing tool, not an editing or data-entry workflow.

Not implemented now: cloud Team administration, identity-provider linking, season leaderboards, supporter notifications, or general administration. A server-only operator email reports each normal or exceptional voting-window opening without delaying the window. Individual ballot data remains private at all times, and aggregates remain unavailable before close.

The single configured operator has a production-only recovery command for an exceptional missed window. Reopening preserves the original window and every ballot, archives the previously published immutable result as an audit revision, hides aggregates, and establishes a bounded effective window. The operator can close an active original or reopened window early; that trusted transaction stops ballot acceptance and publishes a newly aggregated current result. The owner console also provides a separate manual opening recovery for a real final whistle that the provider has not published. These controls are unavailable in supporter UI and are not general match administration.

The same separately authorized operator can register a fixture-specific verified head coach when API-Football supplies valid tracked-Team participant evidence but cannot resolve that fixture's coach unambiguously. The durable assignment records its evidence, reason, operator context, Team and fixture binding, and distinct operator provenance. It never fabricates a provider ID or carries the coach into another match. The normal lifecycle still validates participant eligibility and opens voting only after the participant collection and coach assignment are written together and readiness succeeds.

The same guarded coach operation is available inside the selected Match workspace while the provider still reports scheduled or live, so the owner can prepare final-whistle recovery without asserting `FT` or changing voting state. The owner selects a canonical coach and submits one protected form; the server still performs zero-write preview, one-use authorization, atomic assignment, and durable receipt checks. Voting or protected history locks it.

The public application is available on its current Vercel production URL. Guarded production configuration, bootstrap boundaries, and the runbook continue to control Firebase pilot operations.

On a published match result, a supporter can compare their own submitted player and head-coach ratings with community averages in a compact "Mi voto vs la afición" section. It shows the signed difference (own rating minus average) to one decimal in the existing results order. A supporter without a ballot sees a neutral empty state. This comparison does not score or rank supporters and is unavailable before trusted close and result publication.

## MVP

Partidos prioritizes one relevant featured match: an actually active effective voting window, a live fixture within its bounded kickoff window, the nearest scheduled fixture, then the latest completed historical fallback. A stale provider `live` value on a closed historical vote cannot displace a scheduled fixture. Compact upcoming and recent lists remain below the feature; when that feature is the only upcoming fixture, the tab says it is already shown above instead of claiming that no fixture exists. During an active window the page checks the current anonymous voter's deterministic ballot status through the same trusted boundary as Home: an available voter can enter the rating flow, while a voter who already submitted sees confirmation without a second rating action. Results remain hidden until trusted close.

After a match finishes, voting opens for approximately two hours. An authenticated supporter sees every Herediano player who actually played—not unused squad members—and the match's head coach. They submit one complete ballot. Aggregate results remain hidden until voting closes.

After closing, supporters can browse match results, MVPs, coach ratings, player match history, and player averages. A player's overall Rating App average is the unweighted arithmetic mean of their published per-match averages, preserving equal match weight even when ballot counts differ. Players need at least two published rated matches to receive a team rank; deterministic ties use higher average, more rated matches, then stable player ID. Unranked and historically rated players remain visible. Match and player history is durable and keyed by canonical identity with explicit provider-ID aliases, never inferred from the current roster, display name, or shirt number; confirmed aliases combine historical ratings without rewriting ballots or finalized results, while each canonical player contributes at most one published rating per match.

Jugadores enriches canonical player identity with the current API-Football squad position and identification photo persisted ahead of page reads. The catalog and profile never call the football provider while rendering, historical players remain visible when absent from the current squad, explicit aliases prevent duplicate catalog entries when provider IDs change, and a deterministic local initials avatar replaces missing or unavailable photos.

The public app identity is consistent across browser tabs, bookmarks, mobile shortcuts, and shared links. Spanish canonical metadata describes supporter ratings in the current Herediano pilot without claiming official club affiliation; social previews use a deterministic Rating App card and never depend on authentication, Firebase, ballots, or the football provider.

## Product invariants

- At most one ballot exists for a voter and match.
- Player eligibility requires both tracked-Team ownership and confirmed match participation (`teamId == trackedTeamId && participated == true`), not squad membership, the bench, the opponent, or the current roster. Only the tracked Team's head coach is rateable.
- The tracked team may be home or away.
- Provider final status is insufficient by itself: a normal voting window starts only after valid tracked-Team participant and head-coach readiness, lasts two hours from readiness, and is never extended by retries. A separately authorized, audited operator recovery may establish a new effective window without changing the original timestamps.
- Provider and operator coach identities retain explicit provenance. A match-specific operator assignment may recover only an ambiguous provider coach, must match the tracked Team and provider fixture, and cannot be silently replaced by later provider synchronization.
- Aggregates are unavailable to voters during an active window.
- Final results are generated once by trusted server code from immutable, fully validated ballots. Zero ballots yields an explicit no-votes result; one ballot is sufficient; exact top-average ties remain co-MVPs; the coach is excluded from MVP.
- Provider data is imported through a boundary and remains replaceable.
- Herediano is initial configuration and branding, not a special domain entity.

## Non-goals for the initial MVP

Social feeds, comments, fantasy football, betting, player-to-player comparisons, complex moderation, paid subscriptions, and general-purpose multi-tenant administration are excluded.

## Future direction

The same team, competition, season, match, participant, ballot, and result concepts can support more clubs and supporter communities. Add teams through data and theming; do not fork the domain or create club-named collections.

## Owner administrative recovery

`/admin` is an unlinked, non-indexed Spanish operations surface for the single configured owner. Its bounded dashboard separates open voting windows, upcoming or preparing Matches, exceptional review, and closed voting or published results. Each compact Match card shows supporter-readable status and a state-specific destination without exposing technical identifiers; published results also link to Match administration for the historical provider check. Lineup and head-coach preparation exists only in the fixture detail workspace, which shows persisted evidence, readiness, and canonical tracked-Team Players without calling the football provider. Voting and completed-match evidence used for ratings remains immutable; the finalized provider check writes only its separate source snapshot and discoverable provider identities.

Before kickoff, during play, or after `FT` while voting remains unopened, the owner may prepare and explicitly save an incomplete fixture lineup without evidence fields or a passphrase. The saved preparation can contain canonical Players, fewer than 11 starters, substitutes, arrangement, and locally reviewed participation states. It is private owner working state only: it never creates MatchParticipants, a verified snapshot, eligibility, or lifecycle progress.

When independent evidence identifies a Player who has no usable provider identity, the owner enters the Player's name and optional presentation details; the server allocates an opaque canonical ID. The allocation survives draft save, reload, preview, and an exact apply retry, while the Player document is created only inside verified lineup apply. The owner never types or chooses the internal ID. Allocation signatures use a dedicated server secret. If a bounded signing-key rotation makes a saved draft's internal tokens stale, the editor offers an owner-bound recovery that rotates every internal token in that draft while preserving Player IDs and all lineup work; recovery creates no Player or verified evidence.

Opening the Match workspace restores the owner's saved draft first, then falls back to the strongest persisted fixture evidence: an existing operator-verified snapshot before tracked-Team provider participants. Starters, unused substitutes, confirmed entrants, and unconfirmed participation stay visibly separate. The owner can search the paginated historical Team catalog by accent-insensitive name and move Players by pointer, touch, keyboard drag-and-drop, or equivalent labeled buttons. Each response remains bounded and every matching canonical Player is reachable. Public history, admin roster search, and additive membership repair share one Team affiliation, alias, and canonical identity policy. Online surfaces use the maintained membership index; the explicit offline repair alone exhaustively traverses current squad records, tracked-Team MatchParticipants, and fixture-bound verified lineups. A successful repair records index completeness, normal ingestion maintains memberships, and an absent or stale completeness record makes the catalogs unavailable instead of silently omitting historical Players. Transferred Players and trusted historical Players remain discoverable after population, while opponent-only Players do not enter the Team catalog. This corrected index-backed behavior remains unavailable in production until the reviewed release, declared index, and explicit membership repair are deployed.

Verified confirmation is a separate deliberate action requiring exactly 11 starters, zero-write preview, exact action hash, independent passphrase, short-lived one-use challenge, atomic apply, and a deterministic durable success receipt. The authenticated owner is recorded as the human verification source, so the UI does not ask for evidence URLs, reasons, technical IDs, or typed fixture strings. The lineup transaction reloads the Match, protected history, existing participants, canonical identities, Team membership, and provider aliases. It writes the verified snapshot and materialized participants, removes the owner's matching working draft, and does not change `ratingState`, voting timestamps, ballots, results, or revisions. Exact retries recover the receipt without repeating writes even after later lifecycle transitions. Outside the separately attested manual-opening recovery, the normal lifecycle still owns `FT` confirmation, final participant and coach readiness, and voting-window creation.

Lineup preview and apply return stable safe error codes for malformed input, Match or Team binding, Match state, missing Players, canonical name or membership conflicts, provider alias conflicts, stale drafts, and invalid internal allocations. Harmless case, accent, punctuation, and whitespace differences in a submitted name resolve through the existing canonical name normalization while the stable Player ID remains authoritative. Unexpected failures return a server error with a correlation ID that also appears in sanitized server logs; neither response nor log contains submitted evidence, tokens, cookies, or raw payloads.

The fixture 1551706 incident exposed three defects in the prior implementation: the editor could submit a persisted provider or draft name instead of the selected ID's canonical display name, preview required byte-for-byte name equality, and verified materialization required an optional provider Team ID duplicated on the Match even when the canonical Team retained that identity and the fixture confirmed it. The shared fallback originally converted these and unrelated failures into one generic HTTP 400. The reviewed corrections submit canonical names, accept only narrow normalized equivalence, and recover a missing Match snapshot field from the canonical Team mapping after verifying that the mapped provider Team is present in the fixture. Genuine identity and Team/fixture conflicts still fail closed with stable safe diagnostics.

After the actual final whistle, the owner may use **Abrir votación ahora** when provider status or evidence remains delayed. Persisted `scheduled`, `live`, and `finished` statuses are eligible because the authenticated owner can establish the real final whistle before provider synchronization catches up. Persisted `postponed`, `suspended`, `cancelled`, and `abandoned` statuses are ineligible; correcting one of those states requires a separately reviewed operation. The single final form offers a two-hour default or an exact Costa Rica close, one final-whistle checkbox, and the independent passphrase. Its hidden zero-write preview requires the exact Match, Team, provider fixture, complete operator-verified lineup, 11 confirmed starters, verified entering substitutes only, fixture-bound verified coach, canonical identities, and no material provider reconciliation conflict. Apply rechecks status, every prerequisite, and protected history in one transaction, persists the final-whistle attestation and deterministic audit, repairs a missing materialized coach assignment only when bound verified evidence and the canonical Coach agree, and opens the immutable window at trusted server time. A conflicting assignment fails closed. Provider synchronization may later update football facts but cannot replace this evidence, eligibility, or window.

For the current live or recently finished lifecycle target, the owner can use **Verificar preparación y solicitar sincronización** after preview, passphrase authorization, and exact fixture confirmation. The request is rate limited, audited, bound to the persisted Team and provider fixture, and invokes the same lifecycle service as the scheduled trigger. It can refresh provider evidence promptly but cannot mark `FT`, change `ratingState`, or open voting by itself. The detail view reports confirmed starters, entering substitutes, unused or unconfirmed players, coach readiness, evidence provenance, last provider and lifecycle execution, first trusted `FT` observation, evidence reconciliation, voting readiness, and measured delays when both endpoints exist.

The accepted lifecycle action also creates a durable execution receipt with `requested`, `running`, `skipped_busy`, `completed`, `failed`, or `voting_open` status, separate from Match `ratingState`. A busy or transient post-commit outcome can be checked and retried for a bounded ten-minute period with the original authorization, without another audit or uncontrolled polling. `voting_open` is shown only after the persisted Match confirms an active window.

When API-Football has no usable lineup, player-statistics, or event evidence after `FT`, a complete fixture-bound verified lineup can supply participant readiness independently. Eleven verified starters are sufficient; a bench list is optional. The normal lifecycle also requires a fixture-bound verified head coach before opening voting. It creates no provider IDs or aliases for internal-only Players, keeps other provider failures retryable, and rejects later provider evidence that conflicts with the verified identities, roles, or confirmed participation.

After the reviewed release is deployed, a finalized Match with a published result offers **Comprobar datos de API-Football** in its owner-only detail workspace. The action checks that selected Match's bound fixture on demand and shows the provider starting XI, substitutes with participation evidence, head coach, available entry or exit minutes, provider fixture state, check time, and a side-by-side comparison with the strongest persisted local evidence. It distinguishes missing, partial, stale, available, conflicting, and provider-error outcomes. The normalized provider observation is retained separately from the verified lineup and survives reloads. A less complete later response preserves stronger earlier provider evidence while reporting that the latest response was incomplete.

Late provider Players and Coaches with stable provider IDs become separate provider-sourced identity records. Tracked-Team Player observations also enter the maintained Team membership index so the existing identity workspace can show the provider record beside an operator-created record. The check creates no identity alias and never matches by name. The owner must explicitly reconcile the two records through the protected Player or Coach identity flow, retaining the identity that owns protected history when required. Reviewed aliases then make later provider synchronization reuse that canonical identity without rewriting historical ballots, results, verified lineups, participants, or coach assignments. Manually verified evidence remains authoritative for the completed vote. The action does not change Match state, voting windows, ratings, or published results, add a scheduled historical poll, or change normal lifecycle selection.

After deployment, provider reconciliation records a fixture-bound `matched`, `provider_missing`, `provider_partial`, or `conflict` outcome. `matched` requires complete agreement on canonical alias-aware identities, starter/substitute roles, and relevant participation facts. No usable lineup is `provider_missing`; a strict subset, unresolved internal-only identity, or unknown participation is `provider_partial`; extra or different identities, role disagreement, or contradictory confirmed participation is `conflict`. Missing and partial retries preserve an earlier matched or conflict receipt, while later complete evidence may establish a new conclusive outcome. Every outcome preserves manual truth, and conflicts fail closed without changing verified materialization.

After the reviewed release is deployed, the owner-only Player identity workspace will offer likely duplicate pairs from the maintained Team membership index and a secondary expandable manual search that accepts any two Team Players. Either entry opens the same single review: the pair appears side by side once, provenance and protected voting history remain visible, the safe canonical survivor is recommended, and the owner confirms same person or different people before the existing authorization step. Similar names, normalized accents, abbreviations, positions, and shirt numbers may rank a suggestion but never merge Players automatically. The owner can return to edit the pair without losing it, defer a suggestion, and recover from preview or authorization errors in place. A same-person decision writes only a safe provider alias, missing canonical metadata, canonical Team membership, eligible owner drafts, and its audit; when the survivor has no photo, the same transaction may copy the duplicate's existing stored photo, but it never replaces a survivor photo. Player documents and applied lineups, participants, ballots, voting windows, and final results otherwise remain intact. A different-person decision records a durable reviewed nonmatch and creates no alias. Protected or incompatible history blocks the write and explains which Player must be retained or that manual investigation is required.

After the Coach identity release is deployed with its declared indexes, that same workspace will add a **Directores técnicos** view with the same guided interaction and focus behavior. It suggests plausible manual/provider pairs from normalized or abbreviated names plus persisted Team, Match, and photo context, while leaving every decision to the owner. Its secondary manual search can compare any two bounded, paginated Coach records and enters the same one-pair review. Same-person review creates a provider-Coach alias toward the chosen safe survivor, fills only missing canonical provider metadata, copies the duplicate's stored photo only when the survivor has none, and records the reviewed pair; an existing survivor photo is never replaced by this fallback. Different-person review records a durable nonmatch. Both paths preserve every Coach document, fixture assignment, verified assignment, ballot, voting window, result, and statistic. A same-fixture conflict, incompatible provider mapping, or protected voting history on both records blocks consolidation with a plain Spanish explanation. Deployment and index creation perform no identity mutation or production backfill.

The surface is dormant until the owner Firebase email/password account and all server secrets are configured. Supporter anonymous identity continues in the default Firebase browser app; admin sign-in uses a named in-memory Auth app and exchanges its ID token for a server session before immediately signing out locally.

Admin sign-in displays one accessible busy state from Firebase password authentication through ID-token exchange, server-session creation, and console refresh. The lifecycle request remains visible on every Match readiness panel; ineligible states disable it and explain the persisted reason without changing server eligibility.
